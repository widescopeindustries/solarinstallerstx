## Federal Solar Incentives

### Federal Solar Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

**Credit Amount**: 30% of total system cost  
**Availability**: Through December 31, 2032  
**Program Type**: Non-refundable tax credit

#### What It Covers:
- Solar panels and equipment
- Installation labor costs
- Permitting fees
- Sales tax on equipment
- Energy storage systems (batteries) added after 2022

#### Eligibility Requirements:
✅ Own your home (primary or secondary residence)  
✅ Purchase solar system (not leased)  
✅ New solar equipment (not used)  
✅ System generates electricity  
✅ Own system outright or through financing

#### Example Calculation:
**System Cost**: $20,000  
**Federal Tax Credit (30%)**: $6,000  
**Net Cost**: $14,000

#### How to Claim:
1. Install solar system in 2025
2. File IRS Form 5695 with your 2025 tax return
3. Credit applied to reduce federal tax liability
4. Unused credit can carry forward to future tax years

## Texas State Incentives

### 1. Property Tax Exemption for Solar Energy Devices

**Benefit**: 100% exemption on added property value from solar  
**Duration**: Permanent (as long as you own the system)  
**Eligible Systems**: Residential and commercial solar installations

#### How It Works:
When you install solar panels, your home's value typically increases by $15,000-$25,000. Texas law exempts this added value from property tax assessment.

**Example Savings**:
- **Home Value Increase**: $20,000
- **Property Tax Rate**: 2.0%
- **Annual Savings**: $400/year
- **20-Year Savings**: $8,000

**Legal Authority**: Texas Tax Code §11.27

#### Claiming the Exemption:
1. Complete Form 50-123 (Residence Homestead Exemption Application)
2. Submit to your county appraisal district
3. Exemption automatically renews annually

---

### 2. Sales Tax Exemption for Solar Equipment

**Benefit**: No Texas sales tax on solar equipment purchases  
**Exemption**: Applies to manufacturing, commercial, and residential installations  
**Savings**: 6.25% of equipment cost (plus local sales tax)

**Legal Authority**: Texas Tax Code §151.317

## Net Metering in Texas

### What is Net Metering?

Net metering allows solar customers to receive credit for excess electricity sent back to the grid. When your panels produce more energy than you use, the excess goes to the grid, and you receive credits on your utility bill.

### Texas Net Metering Policies

Texas doesn't have a statewide net metering mandate, but many utilities offer net metering or similar programs:

#### ✅ Full Net Metering:
- Austin Energy
- CPS Energy (San Antonio)
- Green Mountain Energy
- TXU Energy

#### ⚡ Partial Credit Programs:
- Oncor (wholesale rate credit)
- CenterPoint Energy (wholesale rate)
- AEP Texas (reduced credit rate)

### How to Maximize Net Metering Value:

1. **Size system to match annual usage** - Avoid oversizing
2. **Understand your utility's policy** - Credit rates vary
3. **Monitor production** - Optimize usage during peak production
4. **Consider battery storage** - Store excess for later use

## Financing Options

### 1. Cash Purchase

**Pros**:
- Highest lifetime savings
- Immediate ownership
- All incentives go to you
- No interest payments

**Cons**:
- Large upfront cost
- Longer payback period

**Best For**: Homeowners with available cash and high tax liability

---

### 2. Solar Loans

**Pros**:
- $0 down options available
- Immediate ownership
- Monthly payments often lower than electric bill savings
- Qualify for all incentives

**Cons**:
- Interest reduces total savings
- Credit check required

**Typical Terms**:
- **Interest Rates**: 3.99% - 8.99% APR
- **Loan Terms**: 10-25 years
- **Down Payment**: $0 - 20%

**Popular Lenders**:
- Dividend Finance
- Sunlight Financial
- GoodLeap (formerly Loanpal)
- Mosaic

---

### 3. Solar Leases

**Pros**:
- $0 upfront cost
- Fixed monthly payments
- No maintenance responsibility

**Cons**:
- Don't own system
- Forfeit tax credits and rebates
- Longer contract commitment (20-25 years)

**Best For**: Homeowners who can't use tax credit and prefer predictable payments

---

### 4. Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs)

**Pros**:
- $0 upfront cost
- Pay only for energy produced
- Rate typically lower than utility

**Cons**:
- Don't own system
- Forfeit incentives
- Complex for home sales

**Best For**: Homeowners seeking immediate savings without ownership

---

### 5. PACE Financing (Property Assessed Clean Energy)

**Status in Texas**: Limited availability  
**How It Works**: Loan repaid through property tax assessment  
**Benefit**: Transfers to new owner if home is sold

**Currently Available In**:
- Houston (Harris County) - Limited program
- San Antonio - Pilot programs

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: When is the best time to go solar in Texas?
**A:** 2025 is excellent! The federal tax credit remains at 30% through 2032, but many utility rebates are limited and offered on a first-come, first-served basis. Acting now maximizes available incentives.

### Q: Can I combine multiple incentives?
**A:** Yes! You can typically stack the federal tax credit with state property tax exemptions and utility rebates. This combination can reduce your total cost by 30-40%.

### Q: What if I don't owe enough in federal taxes?
**A:** The federal solar tax credit is non-refundable but can carry forward to future tax years. If your tax liability is lower than the credit amount, you can apply the remaining credit to your taxes for up to 5 years.

### Q: Do solar incentives apply to leased systems?
**A:** No. Incentives and tax credits go to the system owner. In lease or PPA arrangements, the solar company owns the system and claims the incentives, which they may pass along through lower monthly rates.

### Q: Are solar batteries eligible for incentives?
**A:** Yes! As of 2023, standalone battery storage systems qualify for the 30% federal tax credit, even without solar panels. Batteries paired with solar are also eligible.

### Q: What happens if I sell my home?
**A:** If you own your system, it typically increases home value and transfers to the new owner. The property tax exemption also transfers. With leases/PPAs, the new owner must assume the contract or you must pay the buyout.

### Q: How long do solar incentives last?
**A:** Federal tax credit: 30% through 2032, then drops to 26% in 2033, 22% in 2034. State property tax exemption: Permanent. Utility rebates: Varies by program, many are limited-time offers.

### Q: Do I need to reapply for the property tax exemption annually?
**A:** No. Once approved, the exemption automatically renews as long as the solar system remains on the property.
